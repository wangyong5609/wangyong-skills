# Interview Patterns Reference

Use this file to calibrate a life interview when the conversation risks becoming shallow, overfriendly, or too career-narrow.

## Public Patterns Adapted

### Life-story interview

Dan McAdams' life-story approach treats a life as a selective story, not a complete chronology. Useful moves:

- Start with life chapters before details.
- Ask for concrete key scenes: high point, low point, turning point, early memory, childhood/adolescent/adult scenes, and one extra defining scene.
- Ask about major challenges, influential people, imagined future, and central life theme.
- Remind the interviewee that the interview is not therapy or diagnosis.

Source: Northwestern Magazine summary of McAdams' script: https://www.northwestern.edu/magazine/winter2005/feature/redemption/sidebar1.html

### StoryCorps-style human memory prompts

StoryCorps uses broad, emotionally accessible questions across important people, happiness/sadness, influence, memory, pride, regret, future, work, family, and growing up. Useful moves:

- Begin with questions ordinary people can answer without career jargon.
- Ask about people and memories before asking for abstract identity.
- Include pride, regret, kindness, loneliness, and remembrance; these often reveal values faster than "what are your values?"

Source: StoryCorps Great Questions: https://storycorps.org/participate/great-questions/

### Narrative journalism arc

Narrative interviewers often warm up before conflict, then search for intention, obstacle, change, and resolution. Useful moves:

- Spend the opening minutes building ease.
- Find the person's desire and the obstacle that repeatedly blocks it.
- Ask how the struggle started, what they tried, what changed, and what they learned.
- Listen without interrupting early storytelling; clarify after the story emerges.

Source: Nieman Storyboard, "The art of the narrative interview": https://niemanstoryboard.org/2024/07/19/narrative-interviewing-nonfiction-journalism-story-arc-character/

### Professional interview craft

Good interview craft depends on preparation, listening for tone and key words, asking "why," and choosing when to depart from prepared questions. Useful moves:

- Keep prepared modules, but let strong answers change the next question.
- Follow up on emotionally loaded words, contradictions, and unexplained decisions.
- Ask concise questions; if the question is long, the interviewer may not know what they are asking.

Source: National Press Club Journalism Institute summary of Terry Gross and Michael Barbaro interview techniques: https://www.pressclubinstitute.org/2020/07/20/terry-gross-and-michael-barbaro-share-interview-tips-and-techniques/

### Semi-structured qualitative interviewing

Semi-structured interviewing uses a topic guide instead of a rigid questionnaire. It combines broad open questions with optional probes for clarity and depth. Useful moves:

- Hold 10-15 broad topic areas mentally, not dozens of fixed questions.
- Probe for clarity, openness, and depth.
- Challenge socially desirable or superficial answers gently.
- Track whether you are getting lived context, meaning, and behavior rather than slogans.

Source: CDC Field Epi Manual, qualitative data chapter: https://www.cdc.gov/field-epi-manual/php/chapters/qualitative-data.html

### Life-design and Odyssey planning

Life design treats career/life direction as a set of prototypes rather than a single correct answer. Useful moves:

- Explore skills, interests, values, and societal needs before job titles.
- Generate multiple meaningful lives, including practical, alternative, and imaginative versions.
- Use small tests to reduce uncertainty instead of demanding a total life decision.

Source: Stanford Life Design Lab example at Michigan State: https://lifedesignlab.stanford.edu/michigan-state

### Founder and long-form success interviews

Shows such as How I Built This often trace childhood, early jobs, accidental discoveries, failures, inflection points, persistence, luck, and the emotional cost of building. Useful moves:

- Do not extract only "success tips"; extract context, timing, resources, luck, and tradeoffs.
- Ask where the person had unusual persistence before external rewards appeared.
- Ask which opportunities were chosen, missed, forced, or accidental.
- Compare the interviewee's actual constraints with the founder's constraints before suggesting an entrepreneurial path.

Source: How I Built This public show descriptions and episode structures: https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/how-i-built-this-with-guy-raz/id1150510297

## Recommended Rhythm

For a 60-120 minute interview, use this approximate flow:

1. Warm frame and current reality: 5-10%.
2. Life chapters: 10-15%.
3. Key scenes: 25-35%.
4. Energy, values, and ability patterns: 20-25%.
5. Resources, constraints, and environment fit: 10-15%.
6. Possibility generation and synthesis: 15-20%.

For text chat, translate the rhythm into rounds rather than minutes. Do not stay in one module for more than 6-8 substantive turns unless the user explicitly requests deeper autobiography.

## Strong Question Types

- Chapter question: "如果把你的人生分成几个章节，每章叫什么？"
- Scene question: "讲一次具体发生过的事，不要总结。"
- Desire question: "那时你真正想要的是什么？"
- Obstacle question: "什么东西挡住了你？是外部条件，还是你自己的某种模式？"
- Cost question: "你为这件事付出了什么？"
- Aliveness question: "哪一刻你感觉自己变得更清醒、更有生命力？"
- Envy question: "你羡慕别人哪种生活？羡慕背后的东西是什么？"
- Repetition question: "这种模式在你人生里反复出现过吗？"
- Counterexample question: "有没有一次你以为自己喜欢，但真正做了以后发现不适合？"
- Future question: "如果五年后你过得更像自己，那一天大概是什么样？"

## Common Failure Modes

- Asking a questionnaire instead of interviewing.
- Staying too long inside childhood without connecting to present choices.
- Letting the interviewee's self-criticism become the whole story.
- Treating passion as a pure feeling instead of a pattern of energy, sacrifice, learning, and repeated return.
- Treating money as dirty or irrelevant; it is not the only goal, but it protects continuity.
- Treating a celebrity founder path as universal.
- Producing directions that sound inspiring but cannot be tested within the interviewee's real life.
